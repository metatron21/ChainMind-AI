## 1. Executive Summary

CHAINMIND AI is a blockchain-token research interface intended to bring together basic on-chain, market, and security signals in one place. CMAI is the token designed for platform-related utility and community or product-contribution rewards on BNB Smart Chain.

The current application is a prototype. It relies in part on external data providers, whose coverage, availability, timeliness, and accuracy may vary. Any rule-based score displayed by the prototype is not a security certification. Holding CMAI does not represent ownership in a company or project, a claim on revenue or dividends, or a guarantee of access to a service unless a specific feature is later implemented and its terms are published.

## 2. Problem and Approach

Researching a newly issued token can require checking contract information, trading pairs, liquidity, and security signals across multiple sources. CHAINMIND AI aims to make these signals easier to review through a single interface.

The platform is intended to surface signals that may help users conduct their own research. It does not trade on a user's behalf, does not require wallet seed phrases or private keys, and cannot guarantee that a token is safe. Missing, delayed, or conflicting information should be disclosed to users.

## 3. Product and Current Status

## 6. Staking and Reward Distribution Policy

### Recommended model

The project intends to reserve up to **20,000,000 CMAI** for community and platform-contribution rewards. Rather than dividing the entire reserve equally by wallet address, the recommended approach is a series of published, fixed-duration reward campaigns. For each campaign, the project announces a specific reward amount drawn from the finite reserve. The full 20,000,000 CMAI should not be assumed to be paid in a single campaign.

To qualify for a campaign, a participant must stake CMAI in the designated staking contract and keep it locked for the campaign's complete **seven consecutive days**. If the participant withdraws before the end, that stake is ineligible for that campaign. All campaign-specific dates, network, token and staking-contract addresses, any minimum deposit, and claim deadline must be published in advance.

For a campaign reward amount `R`, an eligible wallet's reward is intended to be proportional to its eligible CMAI stake:

**Wallet reward = R × (wallet's eligible stake ÷ total eligible stake across all wallets)**

Because every qualifying deposit is locked for the same seven-day campaign, stake amount determines each participant's share. If the project later allows deposits at different times or durations, it should use a clearly documented stake-time calculation instead. A wallet split into multiple addresses does not increase the combined proportional share when all of those addresses stake the same total amount.

### Why this model

This model follows common staking-reward mechanics: Binance Launchpool describes rewards as proportional to a participant's stake relative to the pool total, while PancakeSwap describes farm rewards in relation to a position's share of eligible liquidity. PancakeSwap's CAKE revenue-sharing mechanism also factors both locked amount and remaining lock duration into share calculations. These examples are design references, not endorsements or guarantees of results. [Binance Launchpool reward calculation](https://www.binance.com/en-AU/support/faq/detail/94ed108ce89d44ab8602aa3c476dfb04) · [PancakeSwap Yield Farming](https://docs.pancakeswap.finance/earn/yield-farming) · [PancakeSwap Revenue Sharing FAQ](https://docs.pancakeswap.finance/earn/earn-faq/cake-staking-faq/revenue-sharing-faq)

An equal-per-wallet allocation is easy to explain but cannot establish that one wallet equals one person. A participant could split funds across many addresses and receive multiple equal shares. Stake-proportional allocation avoids that particular advantage from splitting an unchanged total stake, though it does not eliminate all forms of abuse or concentration.

### Implementation and disclosure status

This is the recommended policy for future campaigns, not a claim that the deployed reward vault currently enforces staking or these calculations. Before launch, the deployed contracts must be inspected and the required staking/reward logic implemented and independently reviewed. The project must publish:

- The reward amount for each campaign and the remaining reserve after it.
- Campaign start/end times and the exact seven-day lock and withdrawal rules.
- The eligible staking asset, network, and contract address.
- Any minimum stake and how rounding dust is handled.
- The contract method for recording stake and calculating eligibility.
- The reward snapshot, claim window, unclaimed-reward policy, and distribution transaction.
- The authority that controls the reserve and safeguards on that authority.

Until these terms are implemented and announced, users must not treat rewards as active, guaranteed, or already owed to them.

## 8. Security and Governance

Source-code verification on a block explorer helps users compare published source code with deployed contract bytecode. It is not equivalent to an independent security audit.

The project should document privileged functions, key custody, reward-vault controls, and contract changes; provide a channel for vulnerability reports; and publish a change history. Seed phrases and private keys must never be entered into the project website or shared with support staff.

Governance mechanisms are not described as currently active. Any future governance system should clearly state who may participate, what decisions are in scope, and whether outcomes are binding.
